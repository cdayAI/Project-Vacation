## 2. Management's stated priorities, in their own words

### 2.1 From the Q2 2026 release (primary)

Matt Avril, Chief Executive Officer:

> "Our second quarter results demonstrate the strong progress we have made this
> year, with VPG improving 23% year over year and contract sales growing 22%. This
> was driven by the power of our brands, our strategy, and the execution by our
> associates. Our raised guidance reflects our focus on driving continued contract
> sales growth and increasing Adjusted EBITDA. We also remain committed to
> delivering best-in-class hospitality experiences for our owners, members, and
> guests."

Read literally, that sentence names the priority set: **contract sales growth**,
**VPG**, **Adjusted EBITDA**, and **owner/member/guest experience**. The raised
guidance is framed as a consequence of the first three.

### 2.2 Technology and AI language in the Q2 release

The release contains **no affirmative technology or digital initiative
announcement.** AI appears only in the forward-looking-statements risk factors:

> "...difficulties associated with implementing new or maintaining existing
> technologies; the ability to integrate artificial intelligence ("AI")
> technologies successfully while managing and mitigating related operational,
> legal, intellectual property, data security and reputational risks..."

"Modernization" appears only as an excluded cost in the guidance caveat:

> "The guidance provided above excludes impacts from certain asset sales, foreign
> currency changes, restructuring costs, litigation charges, modernization costs,
> transaction and integration costs, and impairments, each of which the Company
> cannot forecast with sufficient accuracy to factor them into the guidance
> provided above and without unreasonable efforts, and which may be significant."

**This is the single most important sentence in the release for us.** MVW's own
public position is that AI carries "operational, legal, intellectual property,
data security and reputational risks" that must be "managed and mitigated." That
is not an objection to overcome — it is the specification. A platform whose
distinguishing feature is a governed, auditable record is aimed directly at the
risk MVW named in its own 10-Q-grade risk language. Lead with that framing, not
with automation volume.

## 3. Operational metrics management highlighted

### 3.1 Vacation Ownership — the segment that carried the quarter

| Metric | Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contract sales | $545M | $445M | **+22%** |
| Contract sales, six months | $956M | $865M | +10.5% |
| VPG (volume per guest) | $4,477 | $3,631 | **+23%** |
| Tours | 112,721 | 114,402 | **(1%)** |

North America tours **increased 3%** year over year. The overall tour decline is
attributed in the release to "purposeful actions to prioritize higher
profitability and cash flow in the Asia-Pacific region."

### 3.2 The structural fact that matters most to this program

**Contract sales grew 22% while tours fell 1%.**

Growth came from conversion and price per tour, not from volume through the front
door. Every incremental dollar of contract sales in Q2 arrived as a *contract* —
and every contract carries the same fixed back-office burden regardless of how
efficiently it was sold: a disclosure package, a statutory rescission clock, a
funding and titling path, a loan origination if financed, and a permanent
compliance record.

So the back office absorbed roughly 22% more transaction volume on a tour base that
shrank. Full-year guidance implies that continues: contract sales of
**$2,080M–$2,115M** against $956M booked in the first six months means the second
half must deliver **$1,124M–$1,159M** — more in H2 than in H1, against a tour base
management is deliberately holding flat-to-down in Asia-Pacific.

**This is the business case, and it is derivable entirely from management's own
numbers.** The platform's job is not to sell more. It is to let the contract,
compliance, and servicing back office absorb a step-change in transaction volume
without a proportional step-change in headcount or in error rate — and to prove,
on the record, that it did so compliantly. Nothing else in the release makes the
case this cleanly.

## 4. Where an AI operations platform could plausibly move a number

### 4.1 How the ranking is built

Each candidate is scored on two axes, deliberately kept coarse so the owner can
argue with the placement rather than the arithmetic.

- **Impact (1–5):** how directly the workflow moves a metric *management named in
  this release*. A workflow that moves a metric nobody mentioned scores low no
  matter how clever it is.
- **Feasibility without deep systems integration (1–5):** can it run — at least in
  shadow mode (§19) — on document corpora, public reference material, and a
  periodic flat extract, rather than requiring live read/write into MVW's systems
  of record? We do not know MVW's internal systems (§23), so anything requiring
  deep integration is discounted on principle, not on suspicion.

Score = Impact × Feasibility. **The score is a sorting aid, not a decision.** The
notes underneath each entry are where the actual argument lives.

### 4.2 The ranking

| # | Candidate workflow | Metric it targets | Impact | Feas. | Score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Rescission-clock and contract-package compliance assurance** | Contract sales ($2,080–2,115M guidance); avoided rescission/voidance loss | 5 | 5 | **25** |
| 2 | **HOA / association board pack and budget reporting** | Management & exchange revenue (+3%); cost to serve | 4 | 5 | **20** |
| 3 | **Owner-services response drafting over governed knowledge** | Cost per contact; owner experience (named by CEO) | 4 | 4 | **16** |
| 4 | **Loan-file and delinquency evidence assembly (human decides)** | Financing margin (−450bps); loan loss reserve ($122M) | 5 | 3 | **15** |
| 5 | **Maintenance-fee collections correspondence** | Adjusted FCF ($410–460M); default rate | 4 | 3 | **12** |
| 6 | **Exchange member retention correspondence** | Interval members (−2%); ARPM (−2%) | 3 | 3 | **9** |
| 7 | **Sales-presentation compliance QA** | Protects contract sales; FTC / state AG exposure | 4 | 2 | **8** |
| 8 | **Tour-logistics / VPG optimisation** | VPG (+23%); tours (−1%) | 5 | 1 | **5** |

### 4.3 The reasoning, candidate by candidate

**#1 — Rescission-clock and contract-package compliance assurance. Recommended
first workflow.**

Statutory rescission is the highest-consequence deterministic rule in the business:
the window length, its trigger event, tolling, and the proof of compliance are set
by state law and vary by state, and getting it wrong voids the contract (§3 of the
brief). At $2.08–2.115B of guided contract sales, an error rate in the low single
digits is a material number, and the H2 volume step-up (§3.2) is precisely when
back-office error rates rise.

It ranks first on both axes and it is the only candidate that ranks 5/5:

- *Impact.* It protects the headline metric management led with, and it does so on
  the axis — compliance error — where the loss is not a missed sale but a voided
  contract plus a regulatory finding.
- *Feasibility.* The inputs are contract metadata (state, execution timestamp,
  delivery method, document set) and the statutes themselves. Statutes are public,
  curatable, and effective-datable. It needs a periodic extract, not a live
  write-path into a system of record. It runs in shadow mode from day one against
  historical contracts, which builds the golden set at zero risk.
- *It is the thesis in miniature.* Deterministic legal clock, per-state rules as
  data, cited and effective-dated authority, human approval on anything
  consequential, and an audit record a compliance officer can read unaided. If the
  platform cannot do this, it should not be trusted with anything else.

**#2 — HOA / association board pack and budget reporting.**

MVW manages resorts and their homeowners' associations, and board reporting repeats
across many associations on a fixed cadence with a fixed document shape. It is
document assembly from governed sources — the exact shape of §9.

Ranks second because feasibility is the highest available (association budgets and
reserve studies are documents, not system integrations; the audience is boards and
internal managers, not consumers, so the consumer-protection surface is largely
absent) while impact is real if unglamorous: it touches management & exchange
revenue, which grew only 3%, and it removes a recurring manual cost.

Its strategic value is disproportionate to its score: it is a **low-liability
second workflow** that exercises templates, versioning, approvals, and the
knowledge layer without touching a consumer. Good ground to prove the platform on
before pointing it at anything owner-facing.

**#3 — Owner-services response drafting over governed knowledge.**

The contact-centre surface spans every other entity, and the CEO named "best-in-class
hospitality experiences for our owners, members, and guests" as a commitment. In
assisted mode (§19.2) an agent drafts, a human edits and commits, and edit distance
and time saved are measurable from the first week.

Held at #3 rather than higher for an honest reason: **the release names no
cost-per-contact or handle-time metric**, so the business case has to be built from
MVW's internal numbers we do not have. High confidence it is valuable; low
confidence we can tie it to a number management publicly committed to. Inbound owner
messages are untrusted input and must pass the boundary screen (§5.5) before
reaching a model.

**#4 — Loan-file and delinquency evidence assembly. High value, hard constraints.**

This targets the clearest pressure point in the quarter: financing profit down 5%,
margin down 450 basis points, reserve up to $122M from $108M. Impact scores 5.

Feasibility scores only 3, and the constraint is legal, not technical:

- Anything that prioritises, scores, or sequences consumers is a disparate-impact
  question under ECOA and UDAAP (§14 of the brief, §3 of the brief).
- The brief's own rule governs: *"Where an outcome could be adverse to a consumer,
  the human is the decision-maker and the system is the evidence-gatherer."*

So the buildable version is deliberately narrower than the tempting version. The
platform assembles the loan file, extracts and cites the relevant policy and
contract terms, checks the contact-compliance history, and presents a complete
evidence pack. **A human selects the treatment.** The system must not rank or
sequence borrowers in v1. Bias testing and MVW compliance engagement are
prerequisites, not follow-ups — and the Q2 delinquency and default figures we would
need to size this are in the 10-Q we do not yet have (§0).

**#5 — Maintenance-fee collections correspondence.**

Maps to adjusted free cash flow ($410–460M guided) and to default behaviour. The
work is high-volume, templated, and repetitive — a natural fit.

Discounted because it is **owner-facing outbound**, which the brief identifies as
"the highest-liability surface in the product" (§10). It requires the outbound gate,
consent and revocation as first-class data, quiet hours by jurisdiction, frequency
caps, and TCPA/FDCPA-pattern discipline in place *first*. Excellent third or fourth
workflow. A poor first one, because it front-loads the entire contact-compliance
surface before the platform has earned any trust.

**#6 — Exchange member retention correspondence.**

Targets the two metrics moving most clearly the wrong way (members −2%, ARPM −2%).
Ranked lower than the metric erosion alone would suggest because it is outbound
consumer contact (same §10 constraint as #5), because retention messaging is
marketing rather than compliance work — the weakest fit for a governance-first
platform — and because member churn is likely driven by product and pricing, which
no amount of correspondence quality fixes. **Be honest with the owner: this is the
candidate where an AI platform is least likely to be the actual cause of
improvement**, even though the metric looks inviting.

**#7 — Sales-presentation compliance QA.**

Real exposure — FTC and state attorney-general scrutiny of presentation conduct is
named in the brief (§3). But it requires presentation recordings or transcripts,
which means integration we cannot assume (§23), and it edges into employee
observation, which carries the §12 constraints and the unresolved employment-law
questions. Park it.

**#8 — Tour-logistics / VPG optimisation. Highest metric salience; do not build it
first.**

VPG is the metric management led with, so the pull toward this is strong. Resist it,
for three reasons:

1. **It is occupied ground.** On the Q1 2026 call the COO said MVW had already
   "launched a new data-driven tour logistics initiative" and is "implementing our
   proprietary model" for VPG by guest type. Building here means competing with a
   live internal programme that has executive sponsorship — the worst possible
   opening position for a new platform.
2. **It sequences and targets consumers**, which is a disparate-impact question
   (§14) and a bad first exposure.
3. **The 23% VPG gain already happened without us.** Claiming credit for a curve
   that is already bending is the fastest way to lose credibility when it flattens.

Feasibility scores 1 and it sits last despite the highest impact score in the table.
The scoring formula is doing its job here; note that the *reason* it ranks last is
strategic and legal, not technical.

### 4.4 What the ranking assumes — argue with these first

The ranking is only as good as these assumptions, none of which are verified:

1. That contract and rescission data can be delivered as a periodic extract without
   a deep integration project. **If false, #1 drops sharply and #2 becomes the
   recommended first workflow.**
2. That the set of states MVW sells in is known and bounded. State coverage drives
   the entire per-state rules corpus, and the brief is explicit that we must not
   assume state coverage (§23).
3. That "Modernization" is a systems programme rather than a resort or inventory
   programme. The release does not say, and $26M over six months could be either.
   **If Modernization is already replacing the contract back office, workflow #1
   may be aimed at a system that is being retired.** This is the highest-value
   unknown in this document.
4. That outbound owner contact is out of scope for the first release. If it is in
   scope, #5 and #6 rise and the compliance surface expands substantially.
5. That the Q2 delinquency and default detail, once the 10-Q lands, is consistent
   with the Q1 commentary ("120-day delinquencies were up 17 basis points... Defaults
   were unchanged from prior year"). If Q2 credit deteriorated materially, #4's
   impact rises and it becomes a genuine contender for first.

### 4.5 Recommendation

**Build #1 (rescission-clock and contract-package compliance assurance) as the
first workflow, in shadow mode, with #2 (HOA board packs) as the second.**

That pairing exercises every part of the spine — statutory clocks, effective-dated
cited knowledge, approvals with dual control, document generation, and an audit
record — while keeping consumer-adverse decisions and outbound contact out of the
first release entirely. It lets the platform earn autonomy on the ladder in §19
before it is anywhere near a consumer.

The one thing that would change this recommendation is assumption 3. **Ask about
Modernization before committing.**

## 5. Questions this document raises for the owner

Carried into the consolidated question list; repeated here so this document stands
alone.

1. **What is the Modernization programme actually replacing?** $26M over six
   months, described only as an excluded cost. If it touches the contract back
   office, the recommended first workflow needs rethinking.
2. **Which states does MVW sell in?** Drives the entire rescission rules corpus and
   its review burden.
3. **Is there a quantified cost-savings target?** The release does not contain one.
   If MVW has communicated a target elsewhere, the ranking should be re-weighted
   toward cost-reduction workflows.
4. **Do we get the Q2 10-Q credit detail** (delinquency, default, provision as a
   percentage of contract sales)? It is the missing input to ranking #4.
5. **Is the tour-logistics work genuinely occupied?** If the Q1 initiative has
   stalled, #8 changes character completely.
6. **Is outbound owner contact in scope for the first release?** It moves #5 and #6
   materially and expands the compliance surface.
